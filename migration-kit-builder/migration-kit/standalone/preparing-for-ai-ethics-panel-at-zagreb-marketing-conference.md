The ICC Advertising and Marketing Communications Code (2024 Edition) and its 2026 Guidance on AI incorporate the following formal provisions:

Duty of Care and Responsibility: "Responsibility for the observance of the rules rests with the marketer." This includes the selection and oversight of AI tools and algorithms, as the marketer remains the "ultimate decision-maker" regardless of the technology used.
Decency, Honesty, and Truthfulness: AI-generated content must not be "likely to mislead the consumer" or "abuse the trust of the consumer." All claims must be capable of substantiation through verifiable evidence at the time the communication is released.
Identification and Transparency: Marketing communications must be "clearly distinguishable as such." In the context of AI, if the use of synthetic content – such as a virtual influencer or AI-generated persona – is not obvious and its omission would mislead, "clear and conspicuous disclosure" is required.
Social Responsibility: AI applications must not "contain anything which condones or is likely to provoke or stimulate violence" or "play on fear." Furthermore, marketers must take steps to "mitigate bias" and avoid reinforcing harmful stereotypes within algorithmic outputs.
Human-in-the-Loop (HITL): To ensure compliance, the ICC mandates that "human oversight" should be maintained to review AI outputs for legality, decency, and truthfulness before dissemination to the public.
