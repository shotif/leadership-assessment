01 — Executive summary

Six findings the Q2 review needs to confront.

The 2026 Operating Plan has a real strategic backbone — five objectives, eighteen KRs, clear EMM ownership. But the model used to translate that backbone into 159 initiatives is internally inconsistent, and the quarterly steering process around it is reporting backward-looking activity rather than steering forward decisions. Below, in order of leverage, the six things that matter.

  1. The definition of an "initiative" varies by an order of magnitude across the plan.
    A single line can mean a 10-KR cross-functional programme or a single workshop. The plan treats both as equal units, which makes prioritisation, resourcing, and reporting structurally incoherent.
  2. The largest initiatives carry the weakest KPIs.
    The ten broadest-reaching lines in the plan have KPIs like "concept definition / feasibility", "complete scope by end of Q1", or no KPI at all. The bigger the bet, the vaguer the measurement.
  3. There is no programme layer above initiatives, so the same theme is fragmented across multiple owners.
    Nine separate initiatives across six EMMs all touch "Digital Telco" with overlapping scope, no shared status, and no single accountable owner. The plan has no mechanism to detect this.
  4. The People objective (O2) holds 3% of the initiative base.
    Four initiatives against the entire "Build a winning team" agenda, versus 55 for Growth. This is structural under-resourcing of the objective that gates execution of every other objective.
  5. The quarterly steering process is backward-looking only.
    Q1 status field is 76% populated; Q1 KPI achievement, 53%; Q2 forward decisions and focus, 1 of 159 (0.6%). The review process captures what happened, not what to decide next.
  6. Reporting discipline varies by EMM, not by random noise.
    38 of 159 Q1 status entries are blank, but those blanks are concentrated: two EMMs together account for 20 of them. The model does not distinguish between an underperforming initiative and an underperforming reporter.

03 — Diagnosis

Five structural problems
with the model itself.

These are not execution problems. They are problems with how the plan is built. Fixing them would change what the same EMMs produce, with the same effort, in the next cycle.

3.1Granularity asymmetry — the unit "initiative" is meaningless

The plan distributes 159 lines across 32 EMMs at a median of 3.5 per owner. That looks reasonable until you measure the actual scope of each line. Using a simple weighted score (L=3, M=2, S=1, summed across all KR cells touched) the distribution looks like this:

The bottom of the distribution is dominated by what should really be tasks or operational deliverables, while the top includes lines like "Digital telco Family — next Gen FMC" (10 KRs touched) and "Digital telco with Bill of Rights" (9 KRs) which are programmes containing dozens of underlying decisions and dependencies. Treating both as a single "initiative" unit means the plan cannot prioritise or trade off between them. A cancelled micro-initiative looks the same in the dashboard as a postponed mega-programme.

A unit of work that ranges from a single workshop to a 10-KR cross-functional programme is not a unit of work at all — it is a list of unrelated things sharing a column header.

3.2The KPI inversion — biggest bets, vaguest measurement

If the plan worked, the largest initiatives would carry the most precise KPIs, because more is at stake. The data shows the opposite. Of the ten highest-scoring lines in the portfolio, here is what is currently in the KPI column:

None of these are KPIs. They are activity gates ("define", "complete", "prioritize") or pure status notes. They cannot be missed, because they cannot be measured. Conversely, several of the smallest initiatives in the plan (HR, training, single-customer-journey lines) have crisp numeric targets — because their authors had to defend smaller asks with sharper logic. The model rewards vagueness at the top.

3.3Programme fragmentation — no layer above the initiative

Because there is no programme layer in the plan, large strategic themes get fragmented across whichever EMMs touch them. The result is multiple lines, multiple owners, no consolidated status, and no single decision-maker. The most visible example sits inside the file itself.

3.4Portfolio composition — the People objective is structurally undercounted

The 2026 plan distributes initiatives across the five objectives in a deeply uneven pattern. Growth (O3) and Customer (O1) are saturated; Transformation (O4) is well-represented; Reputation (O5) is light but coherent. People (O2) carries four initiatives in the entire company plan. The three KRs under O2 — performance management, leadership feedback culture, and 30% leadership change — are each ambitious enough to deserve their own programme, and instead share four lines.

The most likely explanation is that People-side work is being absorbed into other EMMs' initiatives ("change management", "team setup", etc.) and never surfaces as a named O2 line. The result, whatever the cause, is the same: the objective that gates the execution of every other objective shows up in the portfolio as a rounding error.

The format of the file shapes the conversation: if the only column you have to fill is "what happened in Q1", then "what we should decide next" never gets discussed at the table.

A second symptom is field decay. The file contains a column labelled "current status 21.01." — a tracking attempt that was started, populated for 17 of 159 lines, and then abandoned. Multiple such half-used columns exist in the workbook. The model is accreting fields rather than maintaining them.

04 — The quarterly review

Why the EMM steering meeting
isn't steering.

Three things compound to make the quarterly EMM review weaker than it should be: the file structure is backward-looking, reporting discipline varies by owner not by initiative, and there is no mechanism for the meeting to actually decide anything.

4.1Backward-looking by template design

Of the seven status-related columns in the workbook, six are about the past (Q1 plan, Q1 status, Q1 KPI achievement, the dead "21.01." column, agile roadmap "current status", and a backup history sheet). Only one — Q2 focus / decisions — is forward-looking, and as shown above, it has been filled in for one initiative. The template makes status reporting easy and decision capture invisible.

4.2Reporting variance is concentrated, not random

If the 38 unreported Q1 lines were spread evenly across 32 EMMs you would have 1.2 blanks per owner — within tolerance. They are not spread evenly. Two EMMs (one with 12 initiatives, one with 8) account for 20 of the 38 blanks together. A third has 5 of 11 blank. The model treats these as missing data points; they are missing accountability. The file gives no signal to the rest of the EMM table, or to the COO/CEO consuming the dashboard, that some owners simply did not respond.

4.3The meeting has no decision artifact

A well-run quarterly steering review produces three things by the end of the session: a refreshed status, a list of decisions made at the table, and a list of escalations passed up. The current model produces only the first. There is no field, sheet, or follow-up process that captures the second or the third. EMMs leave the room with the same information they walked in with.

05 — Proposals

What to change
before the Q2 cycle.

Five interventions, ordered by leverage. The first two change the model. The next three change the process around the model. Together they are achievable inside the four weeks before Q2 review without renegotiating any KRs or financial commitments.

Proposal 01 — model

Define an initiative formally, with an explicit S/M/L sizing standard

Today the unit "initiative" carries no shared definition, which is why the same column contains 10-KR programmes and single workshops. Introduce a three-tier classification with hard criteria, and require every line to declare its tier explicitly:

  • Large — touches 3+ KRs across 2+ objectives, has a named programme owner, has >€500k Capex+IDC, runs across all 4 quarters. Maximum ~15 in the entire plan.
  • Medium — touches 1–2 KRs, single named owner, has a measurable Q-by-Q deliverable, runs 2–4 quarters.
  • Small — single KR contribution, single owner, fits in 1 quarter, no separate budget line. Reported quarterly as a count, not as individual rows.

Each tier carries its own minimum KPI standard: Large initiatives must have a quantified target, not an activity gate. Small initiatives are allowed to use binary "delivered / not delivered" KPIs. This single change collapses ~30 micro-rows into pooled "small" lines and forces the seven mega-rows to write real KPIs.

Proposal 02 — model

Add a programme layer above initiatives, with a named accountable owner

Roughly 20–30 of today's initiatives cluster into 5–8 strategic programmes (Digital Telco, FTTH monetisation, AI & data foundations, Customer Experience platform, B2B Cloud & Security, Network Resilience, People transformation). Each cluster needs a single named programme owner whose job is to answer the question "is this programme on track at HT". Initiatives stay as today, but each one carries a programme tag, and the quarterly review reports at the programme level first, initiative level second.

  • Programme owner is not necessarily the EMM with the most lines — it is the EMM most accountable for the outcome.
  • Programme review happens once per quarter as a 30-minute slot, before the EMM round-robin.
  • Programme owner has explicit authority to flag dependency conflicts between EMMs without needing escalation.

This is the single highest-leverage change in this document. It does not require renegotiating any initiative or any KR — it adds a layer of accountability the current model is missing.

Proposal 03 — process

Redesign the quarterly review meeting around forward decisions

The current meeting is a status read-out. Replace it with a three-part agenda that takes the same time but produces decisions:

  • Part 1 (20 min) — Programme health snapshot. 5–8 programme owners give a 2-minute traffic-light read on their programme. No initiative-by-initiative drill-down at the table.
  • Part 2 (40 min) — Decisions and trade-offs. Each programme owner brings one decision they need from the table: a resourcing trade-off, a scope cut, an IT prioritisation, a dependency conflict. Decisions are made and recorded.
  • Part 3 (15 min) — Escalations. Items that cannot be decided at the EMM table get captured with an explicit owner and escalation path (Board, CEO, COO, CTIO).

The artefact of the meeting is not an updated status sheet — it is a one-page list of decisions and escalations, distributed within 24 hours.

Proposal 04 — process

Make non-reporting visible, and treat it as an EMM accountability issue

Today an EMM who does not respond and an EMM whose initiatives are in trouble look the same in the dashboard: blank cells. They should not. Three small changes:

  • Add a single column "EMM response rate" showing what % of an EMM's lines have a Q1/Q2 status entered. EMMs below 80% surface automatically in the steering pack.
  • Reporting compliance becomes part of the COO's standing 1:1 agenda with each EMM, not part of the OKR cycle.
  • Two consecutive quarters of <80% compliance triggers a structured conversation, not a polite reminder.

This is not punitive — it is a recognition that the steering process only works if the source data exists. Right now the cost of not reporting is zero, so the current pattern is rational behaviour from the EMMs concerned.

Proposal 05 — model

Commission a People objective rebalancing review

Four initiatives against O2 is indefensible against the strategic weight the objective carries. Either the work exists and is hidden (most likely — change management embedded in other EMMs' lines), or it doesn't and the objective is structurally underfunded. Both readings need a deliberate response:

  • Surface hidden People work — every EMM identifies the People-related work currently embedded inside their other initiatives, and tags it explicitly to an O2 KR. Expected outcome: O2 line count rises from 4 to 12–18.
  • Identify the gap — once the hidden work is surfaced, the remaining gap against the three O2 KRs becomes visible and can be commissioned as net-new initiatives in the Q2 refresh.
  • Assign a programme owner — under Proposal 02, People should be one of the 5–8 named programmes, with an HR-side EMM as accountable lead.
06 — Adoption path

How this lands
before the Q2 review.

None of the five proposals require renegotiating KRs, financial commitments, or EMM mandates. They are template, governance and meeting-design changes. A realistic path inside four weeks:

WeekActionOwnerOutput
Week 1 Review and validate this diagnosis with COO & CTIO; align on the principle that the model and process need to change for Q2 onward. Sponsor Go / no-go on Proposals 01–05
Week 2 Define the S/M/L initiative standard and the 5–8 programme structure. Identify programme owners. Refresh the Operating Plan template. PMO + sponsor Updated template; programme owner list
Week 3 EMM working session (90 min) to re-tag every existing line into S/M/L and into a programme. Surface hidden O2 work in parallel. All EMMs Re-tagged plan; new O2 line draft
Week 4 Run the new Q2 review meeting under the new format. Capture decisions and escalations as the artefact. First measurement of EMM response rate. EMM table Q2 decisions one-pager

The change that matters most — and the one that costs least — is the programme layer with named owners. Everything else is downstream of someone being accountable for whether "Digital Telco" is on track at HT, whether the People agenda is getting executed, and whether AI & data infrastructure is delivering what the rest of the plan depends on. The current model has no field for that question. The next one should.

The current Operating Plan is a good list. It is not yet a plan. The difference is that a plan tells you what to decide next, and a list only tells you what already happened.
